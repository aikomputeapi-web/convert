# Picture to PDF on iPhone & Android: Simple Steps

Scrolling through your camera roll, you find the twenty photos you want to send to a teacher, an insurer, or a client. You want one tidy document, not twenty separate attachments. That is the whole problem: turning pictures into a PDF on a phone.

Both iPhone and Android can do it without downloading anything expensive. But the routes differ, the default quality settings surprise people, and there is one mistake almost everyone makes at least once. Here is how it works, plus what to check before you hit send.

## Why turn photos into a PDF at all

A PDF is the format almost every office, school, and administrative system accepts without complaint. Photos are not.

- **Attachments bundle together.** Twenty images become one file, so nothing gets buried in an inbox.
- **Order is fixed.** Pages can be rotated, cropped, and sorted before you share.
- **Page numbering and labels stick.** Useful when a form needs "Page 2 of 4" or a photo labelled by date.
- **It prints predictably.** A phone screenshot of a document looks fine on screen and terrible on paper. A PDF prints cleanly.
- **File size shrinks.** Most PDF makers compress images, and one combined file is usually smaller than the loose originals.

That last point is worth remembering: converting is not just packaging. It also lets you cut resolution down to something a person can actually read on a page.

## How to turn photos into a PDF on iPhone

The built-in route depends on your version of iOS, and both paths lead somewhere similar.

**The Photos app route (iOS 16 and later)**

1. Open **Photos** and tap **Select**, top right.
2. Choose the images, in the order you want them.
3. Tap **Share** (the square with an arrow).
4. Scroll the share sheet and tap **Print**.
5. In the print preview, use two fingers to **zoom out** and preview the full page. The preview image on the right expands into a multi-page PDF.
6. Tap the **Share** button in the top-right corner of the preview and choose **Save to Files** or **Save as PDF** depending on what your version offers.

The trick with step 5 is that "Print" is not actually printing. The pinch-to-zoom is what converts the selected photos into PDF pages.

**The Quick Actions route (older iOS)**

1. Open **Photos**, select your images, and tap the **Share** button.
2. Choose **Quick Actions**, then **Create PDF**.
3. Save the file to **Files** or share it straight from there.

**The shortcut route**

If you do this often, the **Markup and Export** shortcut has been the fastest path for years: open **Shortcuts**, search for *Create PDF* or *Export*, pick your photos, and let it write a new PDF into **Files**.

Whichever route you take, the result lands in the Files app. From there you can rename it, move it to a folder, or attach it to an email.

## How to turn photos into a PDF on Android

Android has no single built-in gallery-to-PDF button, but there are three reliable options, and one of them works on every device including iPhone.

**Google Photos**

Google Photos can bundle selected photos into a single document. Select up to the images you want, then use the print or save-to-PDF option offered in the overflow menu. The exact label shifts between app versions and phone makers, so look for **Print**, **Save as PDF**, or **Create PDF** in the three-dot menu after selecting.

**Samsung Gallery**

On Samsung phones, open the album, tap the **three-dot** menu, choose **Combine images** (sometimes listed as **Create PDF**), then save. This is the fastest native route if you own a Samsung.

**Your browser**

Every Android phone ships with a browser, and that browser can already save any page as a PDF. Open a page that displays your photos, tap the menu, and choose **Print**, then **Save as PDF**. It is clunky for a camera roll, but it is a useful fallback when an app will not cooperate.

## Tips before you convert

A few minutes of tidying saves a lot of explaining later.

- **Crop before you convert.** A portrait photo dropped onto a page with full white bars looks accidental. Crop to the document, then convert.
- **Keep resolution high enough to read.** If the page has small print, the PDF made from a heavily compressed photo will be unreadable. Convert from the original, not from a screenshot of a screenshot.
- **Order matters more than you think.** In most tools, the file order is the page order. Sort by date or by document structure first.
- **Name the file well.** "IMG_4821.pdf" tells the recipient nothing. Try "Invoice-March.pdf".
- **Check the final page count.** Open the PDF after saving. Two pages out of twenty missing is a common and annoying error.
- **Rotate, do not re-shoot.** If one photo is sideways, rotate it inside the tool or your gallery instead of taking the picture again.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Printing a physical page by accident.** The iPhone Print route looks like it is about to send a job to a printer. It is not, but the preview is easy to misread. Zoom out with two fingers before you confirm.

**Sending the images as separate attachments.** Some apps offer "share as PDF" and "share as individual" and default differently. Always confirm the recipient gets one file.

**Using an app that wants an account.** Plenty of free PDF tools work by asking you to sign up, watch an ad, or wait through a queue. A [free file converter](https://aikomputeapi-web.github.io/convert/) that simply works in the browser is usually faster.

**Uploading sensitive documents to an unknown server.** Read what a tool says it does with your files. If you cannot confirm where they go, keep them on your device. A browser-based tool that states it converts locally removes the question entirely.

**Converting blurry photos.** No converter invents detail that is not in the picture. If the source photo is unreadable, the PDF will be unreadable too.

**Assuming one page equals one photo orientation.** Portrait photos on a landscape page produce large empty margins. If your tool offers a fit or layout setting, use it.
